# M8 - non-technical explanation

M8 is the milestone where the app stops feeling like "a working prototype" and starts feeling like "a product."

If M7 proved the Android app was real and usable, M8 proved it could feel coherent, personal, and worth coming back to tomorrow.

Before M8:
The app could do important things, but some parts still felt like separate features placed next to each other. It worked, but it did not always feel smooth, connected, or premium.

After M8:
The app feels more like one daily experience. The home screen has a clearer story, the watchlist feels more personal, history/resume feels more natural, push feels like part of the product, and failure states are easier to understand.

## What specifically changed - in plain English

### The home screen became the daily entry point

Instead of feeling like just another page, the home experience now answers the main daily questions:
- what moved
- what matters to my names
- what deserves a deeper look

This makes the product feel guided instead of scattered.

### The watchlist became more personal

The backend still uses a watchlist, but the user experience now frames it more like "My Names" rather than a cold utility list.

That sounds small, but it changes how the product feels. It feels more personal and less like a dashboard tool.

### Market Pulse and Deep Dive feel more connected

Before, these could feel like two separate product modes.

After M8, the flow between them is more natural:
- you see what changed
- you see why it may matter
- and you can go deeper without the app feeling like it suddenly changes personality

### Push became part of the product experience

Push notifications are no longer just proof that a message can be sent.

In M8, the team proved real push delivery to a real phone, and tapping the notification opened the right place in the app. That means push now behaves like a product feature, not just infrastructure.

### History and resume became more useful

The app now does a better job helping users come back to what they were doing.

This matters because a research product should feel continuous over time, not like every session starts from zero.

### Bad states became more honest and less confusing

M8 also improved what happens when things are missing, unavailable, or interrupted.

The goal was simple:
the app should not leave the user on a blank, confusing, or broken-feeling screen.

## What M8 really achieved

M8 did not add a brand-new platform.
It did something more important for this stage:

It made the existing product feel like one intentional experience.

That is a major difference.

A system can be technically correct and still feel weak.
M8 closed much of that gap.

## What M8 did NOT try to do

M8 was not about:
- changing architecture
- rewriting backend ownership
- building new product categories
- doing production hardening

Those belong elsewhere.

M8 stayed focused on product quality, clarity, continuity, and trust.

## The simple version

M8 answers this question:

Now that the Android app works, does it actually feel like a product someone would want to return to?

That is what M8 delivered.
